A business water loss is an emergency from the first hour

A residential leak harms a home, while a commercial leak harms revenue, inventory and sometimes the neighbors above or below. Restaurants face food safety rules, and retail faces damaged stock. The faster water is out, the shorter the closure.

Many commercial buildings share walls, floors and ceilings with other tenants. If the water came from another unit, record that and tell your landlord and your insurer.

What commercial restoration involves

The crew extracts standing water, sets up drying equipment and maps moisture in walls, floors and ceilings. Commercial spaces often have concrete floors, suspended ceilings and large open areas, so equipment is sized to match.

The work follows the extraction and structural drying methods used in homes, scaled to the building. Contaminated losses follow the sewage backup controls.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard a commercial restoration plan should follow. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to it, and ask for the readings that show the structure reached the dry goal.

What to do before help arrives

  • Close the nearest shutoff and tell staff to stay out of wet areas near equipment.
  • Cut power to affected circuits only if the panel is dry.
  • Move stock and paper onto shelves and photograph the loss.
  • Call your landlord or property manager if water came from another unit.

Keeping the business running

Ask the crew to work around operating hours where possible, and to isolate the affected area so the rest of the space can open. Containment and phased drying let a section of the building keep working.

Keep records for a business interruption claim. Note the hours closed, the stock lost and any extra costs. Your policy may include coverage for lost income, so read it and ask your agent.

Multifamily buildings add a second layer. Water from one apartment can reach several below it, and the ceiling page and the wall page explain how those spaces are handled.

Insurance and commercial losses

Commercial property policies often cover sudden water damage, and some include business income coverage. Flood and sewer backup usually need separate coverage. Read your policy and ask your agent.

Commercial water damage questions

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Asbury Park?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available. Shut the water off, protect stock and call.

Can you work while we stay open?

Often in part. Containing the wet area lets other parts of the building operate. The crew explains what is possible for your layout.

Do you document the loss for our insurer?

Yes. Photos, moisture readings and equipment logs are kept and can be shared. We cannot promise any claim outcome.

What if the water came from the tenant above?

Document it, notify your landlord and insurer, and call us. We handle the cleanup. Responsibility is a matter for the parties and their insurers.
