About us

About Asbury Park Water Damage Pros

Asbury Park Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 emergency line for water damage restoration in Asbury Park, NJ and the nearby Monmouth County towns. You call one number, describe what happened, and we connect you with licensed and insured restoration professionals who assess the damage and explain the scope and price before work starts.

This site is written for the first call. It explains what to do while water is spreading, how drying works, what moves the price and what to document for an insurance claim. It does not list a staff, awards or review counts, because we would rather give you checkable information than decoration.

From ring to dispatch

How a call works

A dispatcher answers, asks where the water came from and which rooms are wet, and tells you the safest next step. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available. The dispatcher will not promise an arrival time that nobody can control, and that includes storms and holiday weekends.

On site, the professional measures moisture, looks for hidden water with thermal imaging and writes a scope. You see the scope and the price before work starts. If your insurer needs paperwork, the readings, photos and equipment logs from the dry-out are the records to ask for.

Tell us the source

Pipe, appliance, roof, storm water or sewage. The source sets the safety steps.

Get the next step

Shutoff, power safety and what to lift off the floor while you wait.

Assessment on site

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where the water went.

Scope and price

Both are explained before work starts, in writing.

What guides the work

Standards we point you to

The published industry standard for water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500, and the standard for mold remediation is ANSI/IICRC S520. We name them so you can ask any company you call whether its crews work to them. The professionals we dispatch are licensed and insured, and we do not claim a firm certification on their behalf.

For mold, ask whether your state licenses mold work. For health questions about mold or contaminated water, a physician is the right person to ask. The mold remediation page explains how testing and removal usually go.

Honest limits

What we do not do

We do not do basement waterproofing, foundation repair or plumbing repair as part of a restoration call. A restoration crew dries and rebuilds what water damaged. If the cause is a failing sump pump, a leaking roof or a bad pipe, you will need the matching trade as well, and the dispatcher can tell you when that is the case.

We do not promise an insurance claim will be approved, and we do not bill an insurer for you. We can help you document the loss so your adjuster sees it clearly.

Our content standards

How this site is written

Every page on this site answers the first questions a caller has. What do I do right now, what will the work involve and how do I find out the cost. Pages avoid invented numbers. When a cost depends on the loss, the page lists the factors that move it and says the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Local facts come from sources you can check, such as the water utility, the state insurance and health departments and the city. Where a fact could not be verified, the page tells you to check the FEMA flood map for your address or to ask the city building department, instead of guessing.

We do not publish customer reviews, star ratings, staff biographies or job counts, because we have no records of that kind to show you. If that changes, it will appear here with its source.
