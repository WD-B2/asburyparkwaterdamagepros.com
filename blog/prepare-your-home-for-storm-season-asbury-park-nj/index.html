Prepare Your Home for Storm Season

Most storm water damage can be reduced with a weekend of preparation and a few phone calls.

Call (732) 352-9660

By the Asbury Park Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026.

Storm damage is rarely a surprise in a coastal town. Wind driven rain, surge and heavy downpours test roofs, windows, drains and basements. The good news is that a few inexpensive checks, done before the season, reduce both the odds of damage and the cost of a claim.

This guide gives you a plain checklist and explains why each item matters. It is general advice, not engineering or insurance advice. Your insurer and local officials set the rules that apply to your property.

A checklist to do before the season

AreaCheckWhy.

Inside the house

Move valuables and important papers off basement floors. Store documents in waterproof containers or scan them to the cloud. Photograph each room now so you have a before record for an insurer.

Check the age of your water heater and washing machine hoses. Replace rubber hoses with braided steel ones. Make sure everyone in the house knows where the shutoff valve is and how to turn it.

Insurance steps before the storm

  • Read your policy for wind, rain and named storm terms.
  • Confirm whether you have flood coverage.
  • Ask about a water backup endorsement for sump and sewer failures.
  • Store your agent's number and your policy number in your phone.
  • Photograph the exterior and each room.

If a flood policy is needed, ask about waiting periods. Many take effect weeks after purchase, which is why buying during a storm warning is too late.

When the storm arrives

Keep away from low rooms if water starts to rise. Shut off power to a flooding basement only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely, otherwise leave and call for help. Do not drive into flooded streets.

After the storm passes, photograph the damage and call for help. The storm water damage page explains what cleanup involves, and the flood damage page covers water that came from outside.

Protecting a basement or ground floor

Lower levels take the worst of a storm. Raise appliances and stored items on blocks or shelves. Keep a battery backup for the sump pump if power outages are common where you live. Make sure floor drains are clear, and consider a check valve if sewer backup has happened before.

If you have a finished basement, move electronics and furniture upstairs when a major storm is forecast. Roll up rugs and lift anything that wicks water. It is far easier to move things ahead of time than to salvage them afterward.

Consider sandbags or flood barriers for door openings if your street has flooded before. They help with shallow water but do not stop surge. Never rely on them for a major flood.

Power, gas and safety

Know where your electrical panel and gas shutoff are. Do not enter a flooded room if water touches outlets. After a storm, have an electrician check any panel that got wet. If you smell gas, leave and call the utility.

Keep flashlights, a battery radio and phone chargers in an easy place. Charge devices before the storm. Fill a few containers with drinking water in case service is interrupted.

Make a plan with the people you live with. Decide where you will go if you need to leave, how you will reach each other and who will check on neighbors who need help. Write the plan down and keep a copy by the door.

After a storm

Early drying saves materials. The structural drying process starts with removing water and measuring moisture. Call early, because demand rises after a big storm.

Take care around downed power lines and standing water that may be energized. If you smell gas, leave and call the utility.

Prepare a go bag with medications, chargers, documents and cash. A small kit can make the first night after a storm easier.

Questions homeowners ask

When should I prepare for storm season?

Before the season starts. Checks on roofs, gutters and sump pumps are easier in calm weather.

Does my homeowners policy cover storm surge?

Usually not. The state insurance department says surge counts as flood, which standard homeowners policies exclude.

Where can I check my flood risk in Asbury Park?

Use the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask the City of Asbury Park building department about drainage.
