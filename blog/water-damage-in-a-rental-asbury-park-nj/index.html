Water Damage in a Rental

Both tenant and landlord have a part to play. Here is who does what, and when.

Call (732) 352-9660

By the Asbury Park Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026.

Water damage in a rental raises questions that a house owner never asks. Who should call a repair company? Whose insurance pays? Who owns the damaged carpet and who owns the soaked sofa? The answers depend on the lease, the law and the cause, and this guide only sets out the practical sequence.

We are a restoration company, not lawyers. Your lease and New Jersey law set rights and duties for tenants and landlords. Ask a housing attorney or a tenant organization if you need legal advice.

First steps for a tenant

  1. Stop the water if you can safely reach the shutoff, and tell the landlord or manager at once.
  2. Make the area safe. Stay away from wet electrical areas and keep children and pets out.
  3. Photograph and film the damage and your belongings.
  4. Move valuables and electronics to dry places.
  5. Send the landlord a written report by text or email, so there is a dated record.

Do not wait for a reply before documenting. A dated record of when you reported the problem protects you if disputes come later.

Why renter's insurance matters

A landlord's policy usually covers the building. It typically does not cover your belongings. Renter's insurance covers contents and may help with living expenses if you cannot stay. It costs far less than replacing a household of belongings.

If you do not have a policy, photograph your belongings now and keep the photos. After a loss, ask the landlord what it will cover and put the answer in writing.

What a landlord should do

A landlord who gets a water report should stop the source, call for water extraction and drying and notify the insurer. The structure should be dried before repairs begin, following the structural drying process.

In multi unit buildings, water often travels to neighbors. Check the units below and beside, because a slow drip in one unit can wet several. The ceiling page explains how upper floor leaks show up below.

What to know in Asbury Park

The City of Asbury Park enforces the State Uniform Construction Code through its building department. If conditions in a rental are unsafe or repairs are not being made, ask the city building department how to report the problem.

Neighboring renters in Bradley Beach and Neptune deal with their own municipal offices, so call the office for your address.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration. A landlord or tenant can ask any company whether it follows it, and can ask for the moisture readings that prove the unit is dry.

Writing to your landlord

Keep messages short, factual and dated. State what happened, when you noticed it, what you did and what you need. Attach photos. Ask for a reply by a certain date and say you are keeping records.

Avoid accusations or threats. A clear, calm record is more persuasive than an angry one, and it protects you if the matter goes further.

If the landlord does not respond and the problem is serious, check with the City of Asbury Park building department and consider a tenant organization or a housing attorney. Do not withhold rent or make deductions without advice, because rules are specific.

Protecting your belongings

Lift belongings off wet floors, move electronics and papers to dry rooms, and photograph everything. Wet upholstery and mattresses need quick action. Soaked mattresses and sofas are the hardest items, and the basic idea is to dry them soon or let them go.

Keep receipts for hotels, meals and storage if you are displaced. Ask your renter's insurer and your landlord which costs are reimbursable before you spend large sums.

Moving out or staying

If the home is not safe, you may need to leave for a time. Ask the landlord in writing about temporary housing, about rent during repairs and about when the unit will be ready. Keep copies of everything.

If the unit cannot be repaired reasonably soon, you may want legal advice about the lease. A housing attorney or a tenant organization can explain options. Do not assume you can break a lease or withhold rent without advice.

When repairs finish, walk the unit with the landlord and photograph the result. Check that drywall, paint and flooring have been repaired, and that no musty smell remains. Keep the final photos with your records.

Mold in a rental

Damp rentals can develop mold. Report musty smells and visible growth in writing and ask the landlord to fix the water source. The mold remediation page explains the process. We make no health claims, and tenants with symptoms should speak with a physician.

Keep a log of every message and photograph the growth over time. Dated records help if you need to move or pursue a remedy.

Questions homeowners ask

Should a tenant call a restoration company?

If the landlord does not respond and the water is spreading, a tenant can call for emergency help. Tell the landlord and keep the paperwork.

Does the landlord's insurance cover my belongings?

Usually not. Renter's insurance covers your contents. Ask your landlord what it will cover and get it in writing.

Who can I call about unsafe conditions in Asbury Park?

Ask the City of Asbury Park building department how to report unsafe conditions.
