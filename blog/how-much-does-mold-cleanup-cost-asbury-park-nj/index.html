How Much Does Mold Cleanup Cost?

The price follows the size of the growth, the materials and the testing. Here is what moves it.

Call (732) 352-9660

By the Asbury Park Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026.

Mold cleanup does not have one price, and any company that quotes a number without seeing the space is guessing. The cost follows a short list of factors: how much area is affected, what the mold is growing on, how hard it is to reach and whether testing is included.

This guide explains those factors in plain terms so you can compare quotes fairly. We do not publish prices here because they vary by job. What we can do is explain how the scope is built so the quote you get makes sense.

What sets the price

Cost factors in a mold job.

Why removal costs more than cleaning

Mold on a hard surface can often be cleaned. Mold in porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet usually cannot, so those materials are removed and replaced. Removal adds labor, disposal and rebuilding.

Containment is the other big driver. To keep spores from spreading, a crew seals the work area and uses air filtration. That setup takes time, and it is the reason a small job can cost more than the size of the patch suggests.

The source costs money too

Mold returns if the moisture does. Fixing the leak, the roof or the humidity problem is part of any honest plan, and it is priced apart from the mold work. A cheaper quote that skips the source is not a bargain.

Drying is the other piece. The structural drying process removes the moisture that feeds growth, and skipping it invites a repeat.

What to know in Asbury Park

The New Jersey Department of Health publishes guidelines on mold in the home. They are a useful, neutral reference when a company's advice sounds extreme. We make no health claims, and you can ask a physician about symptoms.

For building work after cleanup, ask the City of Asbury Park building department whether permits apply. Homes in Neptune and Bradley Beach follow their own municipal offices.

How to compare quotes

  • Ask for the scope in writing, room by room.
  • Ask what is removed and what is cleaned.
  • Ask whether containment and air filtration are included.
  • Ask who performs testing, and whether it is independent.
  • Ask what happens if the growth returns.

Two quotes can differ by a lot because they cover different work. Line them up item by item before judging price. For a deeper look at the process, see the mold remediation page.

What a fair scope looks like

A fair scope reads like a plan. It names the rooms, lists what will be removed, describes containment, states what will be cleaned and explains drying. It also separates optional items such as testing and rebuilding from the core work.

Be wary of a lump sum with no breakdown. A scope that lists nothing gives you nothing to compare and nothing to hold the company to if the result disappoints.

Ask how changes are handled. Hidden growth behind a wall is sometimes found only after it is opened. A reasonable company explains in advance how it will price and approve extra work, so you are not surprised halfway through.

Where homeowners overspend

The most common waste is paying for mold work twice. A cleanup that leaves the leak unrepaired fails, and a second cleanup follows. The second most common is paying to remove materials that could have been dried, because drying began too late.

A third is paying for testing that no one needs. Testing helps when a lender, buyer or insurer asks for it, or when you want proof of clearance. Ask what question the test answers before you buy it.

Fast action on the water is the cheapest mold protection there is. A wet wall dried within a couple of days usually avoids mold entirely, and that is far less than the cost of a remediation job.

Timing affects price as well. A growth that is caught while small is a smaller job than one found after a season of dampness. If you notice a musty smell, act on it rather than waiting to see whether it fades.

Insurance and mold cost

Mold that follows a covered water loss is sometimes covered, often with limits. Mold from long neglect is commonly excluded. Read the mold wording in your policy and ask your insurer before work begins.

Photograph the growth and keep inspection reports. A written clearance report also helps if you later sell.

Questions homeowners ask

Is mold cleanup priced by the square foot?

Often as a starting point, but the final scope depends on materials, containment and access. Ask for the scope in writing.

Does testing add to the cost?

Yes. Inspection and clearance testing are separate line items. Many lenders and buyers ask for a clearance report.

Where can I read official mold guidance in New Jersey?

The New Jersey Department of Health publishes mold guidelines for the home.
