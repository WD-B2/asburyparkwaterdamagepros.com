Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak: Save or Toss?

Clean water and fast action can save some items. Contaminated water and time rarely can.

Call (732) 352-9660

By the Asbury Park Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026.

A leak that soaks a mattress or a sofa leaves you with a hard choice: try to dry it or throw it out. Both options cost money. The right answer depends on three things: how clean the water was, what the item is made of and how long it has been wet.

This guide sets out a decision framework you can apply at home. It is general advice, and a restoration professional can assess a high value item.

What the item is made of

Foam, innerspring and memory foam mattresses hold water differently. Dense foam can stay wet inside for days. Innersprings dry faster but can rust. Sofas with solid wood frames and removable cushions fare better than those with particleboard frames and fixed padding.

Natural fibers and stuffing hold moisture longer than synthetic ones. Leather can warp and crack. Upholstery fabric can stain and smell.

Steps to try drying

  1. Stop the water and move the item to a dry, ventilated room.
  2. Blot with clean towels. Do not rub.
  3. Raise it off the floor and prop it so air flows around it.
  4. Use fans and a dehumidifier in the room.
  5. Keep it out of direct heat, which can warp materials.
  6. Check after a day for dampness and smell.

If it still feels wet inside or smells musty after two days, mold may be starting. At that point drying rarely works and replacement is safer.

What to know in Asbury Park

Asbury Park sits between the Atlantic and several lakes, and coastal humidity can slow drying indoors. Run a dehumidifier in the room where the item dries, and keep windows closed on humid days.

The New Jersey Department of Health publishes mold guidelines for homes, and the City of Asbury Park building department can answer questions about repairs. Homes in Belmar and Bradley Beach share the same coastal conditions.

When it is more than one item

If the leak soaked a whole room, the problem is bigger than furniture. Carpet, pad, drywall and the floor need attention, and the water extraction and structural drying process keeps them from spreading moisture.

Check the carpet page to see how wet carpet is handled. The faster the room dries, the more of its contents survive.

Document before you discard

Photograph the item, its label and the damage before you throw it away. Write down the brand, age and cost if you can. These details support an insurance claim for contents.

Ask your insurer how to dispose of the item and whether they want to see it. Keep receipts for replacements. If the item is part of a rental, tell the landlord.

Cleaning and deodorizing a saved item

If you saved the item, clean it carefully. Vacuum upholstery with a clean attachment, then spot clean with a mild detergent. Let it dry fully before using it. Baking soda sprinkled on a dry surface and vacuumed off can reduce lingering odor.

Avoid heavy perfumes, which mask smell without removing the cause. If the odor returns, treat the item as unsalvageable.

Pets, children and bedding

Keep children and pets away from a wet item until it dries. Wash bedding that touched the wet mattress in hot water. Replace pillows that absorbed water, because they are cheap and hard to dry fully.

If anyone in the home has concerns about symptoms around damp furniture, speak with a physician. We make no health claims.

A mattress cover or encasement can protect a dry mattress from future spills. They are inexpensive and easy to wash.

Special cases

Children's mattresses and crib mattresses deserve extra caution. If they were soaked, replacement is usually the simple answer. The same goes for mattresses used by anyone with a weakened immune system or with allergies, since a damp one invites trouble.

For high value pieces, such as an antique sofa or a luxury mattress, a professional upholstery or textile specialist may be able to help. Ask before throwing out something you love.

Time your decision. Give a dried item a smell test after two or three days. If it smells clean and feels dry through the filling, it may be fine. If you doubt it, a replacement is cheaper than the problems a damp mattress can cause. Never put a damp mattress back into a bed frame or cover it with sheets.

Prevent the next one

Keep mattresses and sofas off basement floors and away from exterior walls with leaks. Use waterproof mattress protectors. Fix small leaks promptly so they never reach furniture.

Questions homeowners ask

Can a wet mattress be saved?

Sometimes, if the water was clean and it dries quickly. Foam can hold water inside for days, so check carefully.

How long can a sofa stay wet before it is ruined?

Mold and odor can start within a day or two. After two days of dampness, replacement is usually safer.

What if the water was from a sewage backup?

Discard porous items. They cannot be cleaned reliably.
