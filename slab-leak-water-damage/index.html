Why is my floor warm, damp or my water bill so high?

A warm spot on a floor, a damp slab, the sound of running water with every fixture off and a sudden jump in your water bill all point to a possible slab leak. A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or within a concrete slab foundation.

If you see these signs, close the main water valve and watch your meter. If the meter keeps moving with everything off, call a plumber, then call the 24/7 emergency line for the water damage.

Slab leak water damage is the moisture that spreads through the slab, the flooring and the walls above. Hot water lines cause warm floors, while cold lines cause damp patches, musty smells and lifted flooring.

What a slab leak does to a home

Water finds its way up through cracks and joints. It can lift laminate and hardwood, stain carpet from below and wet the bottom of walls. Because it comes from underneath, it often goes unseen until flooring swells.

The longer a slab leak runs, the more moisture the slab and framing hold. That sets up mold and can undermine soil under the slab, which is a structural matter for an engineer.

Finding and fixing the leak

A plumber locates the leak with acoustic or thermal tools, then repairs it by rerouting the line or breaking into the slab. Restoration is a separate job. The crew removes standing water, lifts affected flooring, measures moisture and dries the structure.

Flooring is the usual casualty. The hardwood page and the carpet page explain when floors can be saved. Drying uses the methods on the structural drying page.

ANSI/IICRC S500 covers the drying of concrete and the materials above it. Slabs hold moisture for days, so readings, not appearance, decide when flooring can be put back.

What to do before help arrives

  • Close the main water valve and watch the meter.
  • Turn off the water heater if a warm floor spot suggests a hot line.
  • Keep rugs and furniture off damp flooring.
  • Photograph the floor and write down when you first noticed the signs.

Where slab foundations are common

Slab foundations vary across Monmouth County, and plenty of Asbury Park homes have basements or crawl spaces instead. Whether your home is built on a slab depends on its age and design, so check your own foundation type.

A water line under a slab can fail from age, corrosion, shifting or poor installation. This page explains the mechanism rather than any local trend.

Insurance and slab leaks

Coverage for slab leaks varies. Some policies cover resulting water damage but not the cost to reach the pipe, while others exclude hidden, gradual leaks. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

Keep the plumber's leak report and photos of the damage. We cannot promise any claim outcome.

Slab leak questions

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Asbury Park?

Yes. The line is open 24/7 and same-day response is offered when a crew is available. Close the main valve and call.

Why is one spot on my floor warm?

A warm spot can mean a hot water line is leaking under the slab. Shut off the water heater and the main, and have a plumber test the line.

Why is my water bill suddenly high?

A hidden leak is one common cause. Turn off every fixture and check whether the meter still moves. If it does, there is a leak.

Do you fix the leaking pipe?

No. A plumber repairs the line. We dry and restore the damage once the water is stopped.

Does my home have a slab foundation?

Check your home's plans or look at the lowest level. If there is no basement or crawl space, it probably sits on a slab. A contractor can confirm it.
