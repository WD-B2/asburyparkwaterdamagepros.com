Downtown Asbury Park centers on Cookman and Mattison avenues, where shops, restaurants and apartments often share walls, risers and drain stacks. A water problem in one space is rarely confined to it. A supply leak above a store can come through the ceiling, and a basement below a business can fill during heavy rain.

This page is for owners, tenants and managers of downtown buildings. Both residential and commercial water damage come up here, so the right plan depends on who is responsible for the space and how the building is built.

What makes downtown buildings different

Many downtown buildings combine uses, such as a restaurant on the ground floor and apartments above. The city's planning documents list a Central Business District redevelopment area downtown. We have no reliable data on how many downtown buildings have basements, so we do not assume it.

What we can say is that mixed-use buildings tend to bring more people into a loss. The owner, the tenant, the apartment above and the insurers can all have a stake, and it helps to decide early who calls whom.

What happened downtown on September 29, 2023

Heavy rain that Friday pushed water farther into the city than usual, and the mayor said it reached Bond Street. A Cookman Avenue music venue reported about three feet of water in its basement, according to the Asbury Park Press.

That report involves one building. It does not show how downtown buildings generally fared, and it is not a claim that every basement here floods.

Checking flood risk for a downtown address

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The city's 2020 hazard mitigation plan says about 20 percent of the land area is in the Special Flood Hazard Area, and downtown buildings should be checked one address at a time.

The FEMA Flood Map Service Center can look up an address. Business owners should also ask their insurer about business interruption coverage and how it is triggered.

Before Anyone Starts Cleaning Up

Shut off the source

Close the nearest shutoff valve. In a shared building, the owner or manager may need to close a riser, so call them at the same time.

Control the electrical risk

Keep people away from wet outlets, cases, coolers and panels. Do not operate breakers while standing in water.

Protect stock and records

Lift inventory, paper and electronics off wet floors if it is safe, and photograph everything before moving it.

Avoid contaminated water

Drain backups and street water can carry sewage. Do not use fans or an ordinary vacuum on it.

Drying a mixed-use building

After water extraction, drying must reach wall cavities and the ceiling void above. In a shared building the crew may need access to the units on either side of the source, so tell the owner early. Structural drying continues until moisture readings come down.

Finishes such as commercial flooring, drywall and acoustic tile may be removed instead of dried. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard that describes how water categories and drying are handled, and it is fair to ask any company how it applies it.

Insurance and permits

Commercial and residential policies differ, and a tenant's policy may not cover the building. Photos, readings and an itemized scope can help describe a loss, but your insurer decides whether it is covered.

The City of Asbury Park enforces the State Uniform Construction Code, so ask its construction office whether a repair needs a permit. The commercial water damage page has more on business losses.

Downtown Asbury Park water damage questions

Which streets does downtown Asbury Park include?

Downtown centers on Cookman and Mattison avenues and the blocks around them. There is no official boundary on this page. If you are unsure whether your address counts as downtown, give your street address when you call.

Is downtown Asbury Park in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified. Zones vary by address, so check yours on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

A tenant upstairs has a leak that is dripping into my shop. Who handles it?

Tell the building owner and the upstairs tenant, photograph the damage and notify your insurer. Responsibility depends on the lease and the cause, so write down what you reported and when.

Do you handle both homes and businesses downtown?

Yes. Callers include apartment tenants, owners and business operators, and the plan depends on the space. Describe the building and what the water touched when you call.

Does business insurance cover a shutdown after water damage?

Some policies include business interruption coverage and many do not. It depends on the policy language and on the cause of the loss. Ask your insurer before assuming it applies.
