Fort Monmouth was a US Army post of about 1,100 acres that closed in 2011. The site spans Eatontown, Oceanport and Tinton Falls, and the Fort Monmouth Economic Revitalization Authority oversees its redevelopment. Our 24/7 emergency line serves properties in and around the former post.

If water is spreading in a building there, close the main valve and call now. Extraction and drying work the same way in older military buildings as in any other.

An unusual service area

Fort Monmouth is not a town. It spans three municipalities, so the right building department depends on which side of the line your address falls. Ask the municipality that holds your address about permits and inspections.

The area includes older buildings, some converted to new uses, and new construction. Older structures can have aging plumbing, flat roofs and large open floors, all of which shape how water behaves.

If Water Is Still Spreading

Close the supply

Shut the main or the zone valve. In large buildings, find the building engineer.

Mind the power

Stay out of water near panels, outlets and equipment. Cut power only if the panel is dry.

Protect contents

Lift records, electronics and stock onto dry surfaces.

Document it

Photograph the loss and note which areas were affected.

ZIP code 07703

The postal ZIP code for the former post is 07703. We list it as a nearby postal ZIP, and we ask for your street address, since a ZIP alone does not show which municipality a property is in.

Long Branch, West Long Branch, Shrewsbury and Little Silver are nearby towns. We mention them only to help you place the area.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers mold. Larger and older buildings are a place where asking whether a crew follows them is a fair question.
