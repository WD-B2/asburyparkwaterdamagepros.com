What a water emergency in Oakhurst looks like

Oakhurst has houses, small commercial strips and schools. For a home, the usual causes of a call are household ones: a burst supply line, a failed water heater, a washing machine hose or a leak from an upstairs bathroom.

Older plumbing and finished basements raise the stakes wherever they exist. A slow leak behind a wall can run for weeks before a stain appears, so any musty smell or soft spot deserves a look.

If Water Is Still Spreading

Stop the source

Close the main valve or the fixture valve. If you cannot reach it, call a plumber at once and then call us.

Protect the electrical side

Stay out of water that touches outlets, cords or the panel. Cut power only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.

Lift what you can

Raise rugs, electronics, paper and small furniture off wet floors and onto dry surfaces.

Photograph the damage

Take clear photos of the water and the damaged items before anything is moved.

Flood, storm and sewage: different problems

Water that rises from outside is a flood damage problem and is treated as contaminated. Rain that comes through a roof or a window is storm water damage. Both can lead to wet carpet, soaked drywall and standing water in lower rooms.

The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance states that standard homeowners policies exclude flood. Read your policy and ask your agent how it treats rain, sewer backup and flood.
