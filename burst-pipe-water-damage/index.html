Burst pipe water damage happens when a pressurized supply line splits and sprays or floods a room. A burst pipe moves far more water than a slow drip, so every minute counts. Call the 24/7 emergency line as soon as the water is off. A dispatcher can begin lining up a crew while you work through the steps below.

Close the main water valve first. In most homes it is where the water line enters, near the meter or in the basement. Then open a lower faucet to drain the pipes. Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets, and cut power only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.

A burst pipe is a Category 1 emergency until it is not

Water from a supply line starts out clean, which the S500 standard calls Category 1. That window closes. As water soaks insulation, carpet and the dust inside walls, it picks up contamination, and the longer it sits the more material has to come out instead of being dried.

That is why the shutoff and the first hours matter more than anything else you do. Fast water removal keeps more of your drywall, flooring and cabinetry salvageable.

What usually fails

Pipes burst at joints, at old corroded sections and wherever freezing weakened them. A pipe that split because of cold is a frozen pipe problem with its own thaw risks. A washing machine hose or a fitting under the sink belongs to the appliance leak page or the kitchen page.

Pinhole leaks inside a wall can run for weeks before a stain appears. If your water bill jumped with no change in how you use water, a hidden leak is a real possibility, and a plumber should check the line.

After the water is off: what the work involves

First, water extraction removes the standing water. Then the crew opens wet walls and floors where moisture is trapped, because drywall and insulation hold water long after the surface looks dry. Infrared imaging and moisture meters show where the water traveled.

Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the structure, a process explained on the structural drying page. When readings reach the dry standard, repair begins on the walls, flooring and finishes.

What to do before help arrives

  • Close the main water valve and open a low faucet to drain the lines.
  • Stay out of water that touches outlets, and cut power only if the panel is dry.
  • Lift valuables off the floor and photograph the damage.
  • Do not run a household vacuum through standing water.

Why local water pressure and older plumbing matter

New Jersey American Water supplies Asbury Park, and a street-side failure on the utility side of your meter is the utility's repair, not yours. If water is surfacing in the yard or the street, call the utility first. Anything inside your shutoff valve is yours to stop.

A plumber fixes the pipe, and a restoration crew fixes the damage. You usually need both, and the order is plumber to stop the leak, then drying. Do not hold up drying while you wait for the pipe repair if the water is already off.

Insurance after a burst pipe

A sudden pipe burst is the kind of loss homeowners policies commonly cover, though the pipe repair itself and gradual leaks are often treated differently. Ask your insurer what applies, and report the loss promptly.

Take photos of the burst section, the wet rooms and every damaged item. Keep the plumber's invoice. These records help your adjuster, but we cannot promise any claim outcome.

Burst pipe questions

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Asbury Park?

Yes. The emergency line is open 24/7, every day of the year. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available. Shut off the main valve, keep people away from wet outlets and call.

Where is my main water shutoff?

It is usually where the water line enters the building, often near the meter, in a basement or in a utility closet. Turn the handle clockwise until the water stops. Finding it before an emergency saves minutes.

Should I call a plumber or a restoration company first?

Stop the water first. Then call both. A plumber repairs the failed pipe, and the restoration crew removes water and dries the structure. If the water is already off, drying should not wait for the pipe repair.

Will the walls need to be opened?

Often some of them do. Wet insulation and drywall hold moisture, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show where. Opening a small section is cheaper than waiting for hidden mold.

Is a pinhole leak inside a wall covered by insurance?

It depends on your policy and how long the leak ran. Sudden damage is often covered, and slow leaks often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and document what you find.
