Wall and drywall water damage repair is the drying and replacement of wet wall board, insulation and framing. Drywall acts like a wick. Water at the base climbs the board, and a leak inside the cavity soaks both faces. If your Asbury Park walls are stained, bubbling or soft, call us to check how far the moisture went.

Turn off the water supply if a pipe is the source. Do not poke into a wet wall near wiring. Move furniture away from the wall and photograph the damage before anything is opened.

Signs of wet walls

Staining, peeling paint, bubbling, a soft feel when pressed, warped baseboards and a musty smell all point to moisture. Some walls look fine on the surface while the cavity behind them is wet. Moisture meters and thermal cameras find the wet areas without opening the wall.

A wall that feels cold or damp to the touch on a warm day is worth checking. So is any wall that shares a space with a bathroom, kitchen or laundry.

How the wall is dried and repaired

The crew uses meters to map moisture, then cuts a flood cut a few inches above the visible line when board must go. Wet insulation comes out, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavity, as described on the structural drying page.

After the readings are dry, new board goes in. The seams are taped and mudded, then sanded, primed and painted. Painting a few feet of wall can leave a mismatch, so the repair often extends to a full wall or corner.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for drying walls after water. It supports drying board in place when it is clean water and the board is firm, and removing it when it is soft or contaminated.

Where wall water comes from

Walls get wet from supply and drain leaks, such as the ones on the burst pipe page, from a roof or window, from ceiling leaks that run down and from flood water that wicks up. Each has its own fix.

Plaster walls in older Asbury Park buildings dry differently from modern drywall, and lath behind plaster can hold water. The crew adjusts for the material.

Do not paint or caulk over a damp wall. The moisture stays, and the finish fails.

Electrical outlets and switches in a wet wall are a concern. Water can reach boxes and wiring inside the cavity, so ask for an electrician to check them before power is restored to a badly soaked wall. Baseboards, trim and doors that touch the wet area can swell and stick, so they are inspected too. Keep notes on every room where you saw staining, since stains on one side of a wall often have a wet cavity behind them.

Wall and drywall questions

Can wet drywall be dried instead of replaced?

Sometimes, if it was clean water and the board is firm. Soft or crumbling board is replaced.

How do you find water inside a wall?

Moisture meters and thermal cameras show wet areas. A small test opening can confirm.

Will the repair match my paint?

The crew matches as closely as possible. Repainting a full wall gives a cleaner result.

Is a bubbling wall a sign of mold?

It signals moisture. Mold may or may not be present. See the mold remediation page for growth.

Does insurance cover wall repair?

Sudden leaks often are covered. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
